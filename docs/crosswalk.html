ENOUGH Crosswalk

A crosswalk — a side-by-side comparison — of where Maryland's ENOUGH grantee communities sit inside other public investment programs. It answers, program by program: how many ENOUGH communities could also tap this funding tool?

Map ENOUGH Crosswalk Methodology

What this page shows — in plain language

The ENOUGH Act is a Maryland grant program that funds local organizations to serve neighborhoods with high child poverty. Several other federal and state programs also steer investment into distressed places — through tax credits and reinvestment dollars. This page checks how much those programs and ENOUGH's grantee communities cover the same ground, so grantees and the state can see where funding tools can be braided together.

ENOUGH community
One active ENOUGH grantee organization and the set of neighborhoods it serves.
Grantee tract
A census tract (a small, official neighborhood-sized geography) that a grantee serves. The 28 communities serve 111 tracts.
“Overlaps”
A community overlaps a program if any of its tracts fall inside that program's geography. We also show the tract-level share, which is the stricter, more honest measure of reach.
Out of what?
Statewide programs are scored against all 28 communities and 111 tracts. The two Baltimore City programs (BVRI and the DHCD Impact Areas) are scored against only the 11 communities and 46 tracts inside the city — a grantee in Frederick or Salisbury cannot contain a Baltimore City vacant property, so counting it as a miss would invent a gap that is really just a jurisdiction boundary. Each of those cards says so and lists the 11 communities.
